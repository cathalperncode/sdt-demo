Seeing motion in noise

A signal detection theory demo · about 10 minutes

You'll watch clouds of moving dots and decide, over and over, whether some of them were moving together. At the end you'll get a report of how sensitive you were and how biased your answers were, and you'll see how those numbers changed with every answer you gave.

Instructions

In this task you'll see a circle of moving dots.

On some trials, a group of the dots move together in the same direction.
On other trials, all of the dots move in random directions.

Your job is to decide which one you saw.

These examples are easy. In the task, many trials will be harder.

Next you'll try one example trial.

In signal detection terms, answering “moving together” is a ‘Present’ response and “moving random” is an ‘Absent’ response. d′ (sensitivity) is how well you could tell signal trials from noise trials. 0 means no better than guessing; bigger is better. c (criterion, or bias) is how willing you were to say “Present.” Negative c means you leaned toward “Present” (liberal), positive c means you leaned toward “Absent” (conservative), and 0 means no lean either way.
